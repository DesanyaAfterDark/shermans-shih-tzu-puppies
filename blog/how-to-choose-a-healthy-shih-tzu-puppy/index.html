How to Choose a Healthy Shih Tzu Puppy: Questions to Ask a Breeder

Bringing home a Shih Tzu puppy is exciting, and it's a commitment that can last well over a decade. The best start is a healthy puppy from a breeder who is open, patient, and happy to answer your questions.

This guide covers what a healthy puppy looks like, which records to ask for, meeting the parents, questions to ask, red flags, and what to bring on pickup day.

What a Healthy Shih Tzu Puppy Looks Like

Spend a few quiet minutes watching and handling the puppies. The RSPCA and AKC point to the same basic signs of good health.

Body and Coat

  • Eyes: Bright and clear, with no redness or discharge.
  • Ears: Clean, with no bad smell, redness, or discharge.
  • Nose and breathing: No nasal discharge. Breathing should be quiet and easy, with no coughing or wheezing.
  • Skin and coat: Clean, soft, and shiny, with no red or bare patches and no fleas.
  • Legs: Strong and steady, with no limping.
  • Bottom: Clean and dry under the tail.
  • Body condition: Not too plump and not too bony, similar to the rest of the litter.

Behavior

A healthy puppy is bright, active, and friendly. The Scottish SPCA says healthy puppies are curious about you and comfortable being handled. The RSPCA suggests passing on a puppy that seems timid or afraid, since that can point to behavior problems later.

Health Records, Vet Checks, and Vaccinations

VCA Animal Hospitals says a responsible breeder works with a veterinarian who examines each puppy and provides vaccinations and parasite control. The breeder should readily share a complete medical record for each pup.

Vaccinations

Puppies get their shots in a series over several months. The AKC notes there's no single schedule for every dog, so your own vet will decide what your puppy needs. Ask which vaccines and deworming treatments your puppy has had, and when, and bring those records to your vet.

Health Screening for the Parents

Health testing is done on the parent dogs before breeding. For Shih Tzu, the American Shih Tzu Club and the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA) list an eye exam by a board-certified veterinary ophthalmologist and a patella (kneecap) evaluation as the required tests for a Canine Health Information Center (CHIC) number. Hip and heart evaluations are optional but recommended.

Ask what screening the parents have had and whether you can see results. OFA notes its certifications can be looked up on its website, and warns buyers to be wary of results that can't be verified.

Meeting the Parents

Seeing the mother tells you a lot about what your puppy may grow into. Cornell University's College of Veterinary Medicine notes that many breeders don't keep the father on site, but you should be able to meet the mother.

VCA says the parents should be friendly and social, and the puppies should share that demeanor. PDSA adds that the mother should interact normally with the litter and seem interested in her puppies. OFA also recommends puppies raised in the home, around people and everyday household sounds.

Questions to Ask a Shih Tzu Breeder

These questions draw on advice from the AKC, OFA, VCA, and Cornell:

  • How long have you raised Shih Tzu, and what health issues affect the breed?
  • What health screening have the parents had? May I see the results?
  • Can I meet the mother? What can you tell me about the father?
  • Where are the puppies raised, and how are they socialized?
  • Which vaccines and deworming has my puppy had?
  • Do you provide a written contract or health guarantee?
  • What happens if I can't keep the dog someday?
  • What does my puppy eat, and what's the daily routine?
  • Can I contact you with questions after pickup?

Expect questions back. The AKC says good breeders ask plenty of questions to make sure their puppies go to good homes. The American Shih Tzu Club lists common ones, such as whether you have children and whether you understand the grooming the breed needs.

Red Flags to Watch For

Trust your instincts. These warning signs come from the AKC, VCA, the FTC, and the Better Business Bureau (BBB):

  • Shipping sight unseen. The BBB advises against buying from people across the country who say they'll ship your puppy. Scammers often add surprise fees for crates, shots, or shipping.
  • No phone or video contact. The AKC says a reputable breeder will talk with you by phone or video chat, if not in person, before selling you a puppy.
  • Pressure to decide fast. The AKC says to be careful if a seller rushes your decision or your deposit, and the BBB lists high-pressure tactics as a scam sign.
  • "Parking lot" handoffs. VCA advises avoiding them, along with sellers who won't show where the puppy was raised.
  • Risky payment requests. The FTC says only scammers insist on gift cards, payment apps, cryptocurrency, or wire transfers.
  • Missing paperwork. VCA lists no vet records, contract, or references among puppy mill signs.
  • Copied photos. The AKC suggests searching a listing's text and photos to see if they appear elsewhere.

Pickup Day: What to Bring and Expect

The AKC says to expect a puppy to go home at about 8 to 12 weeks old, after time to mature with its mother and littermates.

What to Bring

The AKC GoodDog! Helpline suggests:

  • A crate with a towel, plus extra towels
  • Bottled water and a water dish
  • A collar and leash sized for a puppy
  • Waste bags, paper towels, cleaner, and a trash bag
  • A chew toy

Use the crate rather than your lap. The Helpline says it's safer, and motion sickness on a first car ride is normal for puppies.

What to Expect

Plan to leave with your puppy's vet and vaccination records. VCA says breeders typically send home a small supply of the puppy's food to avoid a sudden diet change.

Frequently Asked Questions

What age should a Shih Tzu puppy go home?

The AKC says about 8 to 12 weeks, so the puppy has time to mature with its mother and littermates.

Is it safe to have a Shih Tzu puppy shipped?

The BBB advises against buying from people across the country who say they'll ship your puppy, and many puppy scams involve surprise shipping fees. Meeting the puppy in person or on a live video call is a safer start.

What paperwork should come with my puppy?

Ask for vet, vaccination, and deworming records, plus any contract or health guarantee.

Are "teacup" or "imperial" Shih Tzu a real type?

No. The American Shih Tzu Club calls these labels a myth used to sell undersized and often unhealthy dogs.

Should a Shih Tzu puppy be a holiday surprise?

The American Shih Tzu Club says experienced breeders advise against it. The person caring for the dog should help choose the puppy, and a quiet time is better for coming home.
