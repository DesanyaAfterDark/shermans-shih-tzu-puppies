If you've searched "Shih Tzu for sale under $300," you're not alone. Everyone wants a fair deal on a new family member. But when it comes to puppies, a very low price can cost you a lot more in the end. Here's an honest look at what a Shih Tzu puppy costs, the warning signs consumer agencies point to, and how we price our puppies at Sherman's Shih Tzu Puppies.

What Does a Shih Tzu Puppy Cost?

Prices vary by breeder, region and registration. What matters more than any single number is what the price includes. Before you compare listings, ask each seller:

  • Is the puppy registered, and with which registry?
  • Have the parents been health or DNA tested?
  • Is there a written health guarantee?
  • What vet care and records come with the puppy?
  • Can I visit, or at least do a live video call?

The Better Business Bureau (BBB) recommends researching the breed to get a sense of a fair market price, so an unusually low price stands out.

Why Very Cheap Shih Tzu Listings Are a Red Flag

Scammers use low prices as bait

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) warns that scammers post fake puppy ads, sometimes advertising purebred puppies for a few hundred dollars when they often cost thousands. Once you pay, the puppy may never arrive, and may not even exist.

The BBB lists "popular breeds available for incredibly low prices" as a top pet-scam red flag. It also cautions that if a purebred dog is advertised for free or at a deep discount, it could be a fraudulent offer.

The "extra fees" trap

According to the BBB, pet scammers often keep asking for more money after the first payment: extra charges for cages, vaccinations or shipping, sometimes with pressure that the animal will be harmed if you don't pay.

How you're asked to pay matters

The FTC says only scammers insist you pay with gift cards, a payment app, cryptocurrency or a wire transfer service, because it's hard to get that money back. The BBB gives the same warning. A good rule with any seller: use a traceable payment method and keep your receipts, and never send gift cards, wire transfers or payment-app money to a stranger you haven't met.

No health testing behind the price

Not every inexpensive puppy is a scam. But real costs go into raising puppies responsibly. The American Kennel Club (AKC) says responsible breeders screen for health issues and that both parents should be tested. A study comparing online puppy ads with AKC parent club breeders found the parent club breeders were more likely to do breed-specific health testing, and their puppies cost more on average.

Other Puppy Scam Warning Signs

The FTC and BBB both point to these signs:

  • No visit or video call. Legitimate breeders encourage an in-person or video visit, according to the FTC.
  • Recycled photos. Do a reverse image search. If the same puppy photos show up on other websites, BBB says it's likely a fraud.
  • Pressure to act fast. The FTC notes scammers push you to pay before the puppy "goes to another home."
  • Shipping only. BBB advises against buying a pet you can't visit before you pay and pick up yourself.

If you think you've found a scam, report it at ReportFraud.ftc.gov or BBB Scam Tracker.

The purchase price is only the start. Plan for vet visits, puppy food, a crate, toys, and grooming. That long Shih Tzu coat needs regular brushing, and many owners use a professional groomer.
